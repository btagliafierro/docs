    Flexible gate (with FEA)¶

    This example reproduces a classic hydroelastic benchmark: a shallow water column is held back by a thin elastic gate, clamped at its top edge and free at the bottom, which bends under the hydrostatic and hydrodynamic loading of the water. This configuration follows the well-known flexible-gate dam-break experiments used to validate fluid-structure interaction solvers, such as those of Antoci, Gallati & Sibilla (2007).

    What's covered in this tutorial:

    • Parametric case definition, with the particle spacing itself derived from the beam thickness and a target resolution
    • An open outlet boundary on an initially dry downstream channel
    • Material properties assigned to boundary markers from an external property file (propertyfile/links)
    • Mixed use of norplane (for flat rigid walls) and norparts (for the flexible plate) to compute mDBC normals
    • Coupling with Project Chrono's FEA module, with the beam clamped at the top and free at the bottom
    • Multiple free-surface (SWL) gauges to monitor the reservoir water level over time

    Case configuration¶

    Physical setup¶

    The simulation represents a small 2-D–like tank with:

    • Reservoir: 0.10 m long × 0.14 m deep water column
    • Gate: a thin elastic plate, 0.079 m tall, clamped at the top and free at the bottom, standing at the downstream end of the reservoir

    For this case, we use:

    • Fluid mkfluid=0 for the reservoir water, mkfluid=2 for the downstream outlet zone
    • Boundary mkbound=1 for the upstream (back) wall, mkbound=2 for the rigid wall above the gate, mkbound=3 for the tank floor, and mkbound=50 for the flexible gate.

    Parametric Geometry Definition¶

    <predefinition>                
    <newvarcte beamLength="0.079" />
    <newvarcte N="4" />
    <newvarcte waterH="0.140" />
    <newvarcte tbeam="0.005" />  
    <newvarcte tdp="0.004" />
    <newvarcte res="#tdp/N" />  
    <newvarcte baseOffset="#res" />
</predefinition>

    Beam-driven resolution: the particle spacing (res) is computed as tdp/N = 0.004/4 = 0.001 m, ensuring an even number of particles (N=4) will always resolve a reference thickness (tdp).

    Base offset: baseOffset, equal to one particle spacing, is used to lift the base of the flexible beam slightly above the tank floor, avoiding particle overlap between the two independently drawn geometries.

    Domain setup¶

    <definition dp="#res">
    <pointmin x="-1.000000" y="0.000000" z="-1.000000" />
    <pointmax x="1.000000" y="0.000000" z="0.500000" />
</definition>

    Particle spacing: dp = 0.001 m, fine enough to resolve the thin (5 mm) elastic gate with several particles across its thickness.

    Outlet buffer (downstream channel)¶

    <setmkfluid mk="2" />
<drawbox>
    <boxfill>right</boxfill>
    <point x="0" y="-0.5" z="0" />
    <size x="0.3" y="1" z="0.02" />
</drawbox>

    Outlet positioning: boxfill="right" creates a thin strip of fluid particles only at the right-hand edge of this 0.30 m box, marked as mkfluid=2. This thin strip acts as the seed for the outlet buffer zone (defined later in the inout block); the rest of the downstream channel starts empty, i.e., dry, until the reservoir water reaches it.

    Reservoir fluid¶

    <setmkfluid mk="0" />
<drawbox>
  <boxfill>solid</boxfill>
  <point x="0.00000" y="-0.500000" z="0.0" />
  <size x="0.100000" y="1.000000" z="#waterH" />
</drawbox>

    The reservoir is drawn as a solid block of fluid (mkfluid=0), 0.10 m long and waterH = 0.14 m deep, occupying the upstream end of the domain.

    Rigid boundaries¶

    <setmkbound mk="1" />
<drawbox>
  <boxfill>solid</boxfill>
  <point x="#-tbeam" y="-0.500000" z="0.000000" />
  <size x="#tbeam" y="1.000000" z="0.170000" />
</drawbox>
<setmkbound mk="2" />
<drawbox>
  <boxfill>solid</boxfill>
  <point x="0.10000" y="-0.500000" z="#beamLength" />
  <size x="#tbeam" y="1.000000" z="0.087000" />
</drawbox>
<setmkbound mk="3" />
<drawbox>
  <boxfill>solid</boxfill>
  <point x="#-tbeam" y="-0.500000" z="#-tbeam" />
  <size x="0.31" y="1.000000" z="#tbeam" />
</drawbox>

    Back wall (mkbound=1): a rigid wall, one beam-thickness wide, forming the closed upstream end of the reservoir.

    Upper wall above the gate (mkbound=2): a rigid wall segment starting exactly where the flexible gate ends (z = beamLength) and continuing up to 0.166 m, so the full-height barrier between the reservoir and the downstream channel is rigid above the gate and flexible below it.

    Floor (mkbound=3): a thin rigid slab spanning the whole domain length (0.31 m), forming the tank bed beneath both the reservoir and the downstream channel.

    Flexible gate¶

    <setmkbound mk="50" />
<drawbox>
  <boxfill>solid</boxfill>
  <point x="0.100" y="-0.5" z="#baseOffset" />
  <size x="0.005" y="1.0" z="#beamLength" />
</drawbox>
<shapeout file="beam"/>

    The gate (mkbound=50) occupies the same plane as the rigid wall above it, but only up to beamLength = 0.079 m in height, starting from baseOffset just above the floor.

    Material properties¶

    <properties>
  <propertyfile file="Floating_Materials.xml" path="materials" />
  <links>
    <link mkbound="0" property="steel" comment="Property for the wall" />
    <link mkbound="50" property="steel" comment="Property for the beam" />
  </links>
</properties>

    Try to see if we can remove this material if it is not needed for the simulation.

    Boundary normals¶

    For the definition of the mirroring surfaces, this case utilizes automatic features from Gencase.

    <normals active="true">     
    <norplane mkbound="1">
        <point auto="true" />
        <normal x="1" y="0" z="0" />
        <maxdisth v="3" />
    </norplane>         
    <norplane mkbound="2">
        <point auto="true" />
        <normal x="-1" y="0" z="0" />
        <maxdisth v="3" />
    </norplane>             
    <norplane mkbound="3">
        <point auto="true" />
        <normal x="0" y="0" z="1" />
        <maxdisth v="3" />
    </norplane> 
    <norparts mkbound="50">
        <maxdisth v="3" />
    </norparts>         
</normals>

    Flat walls (norplane): since the back wall, the upper wall and the floor are all flat, their mDBC normals are given directly as constant vectors — pointing into the reservoir (+\(x\)) for the back wall, back towards the reservoir (−\(x\)) for the upper wall, and upward (+\(z\)) for the floor.

    Flexible plate (norparts): because the gate deforms during the simulation, its normals cannot be fixed in advance and are instead computed directly from the particle distribution (as in the earlier flexible-beam case), up to a distance of 3\(H\).

    Open boundary (outlet zone)¶

    <inout active="true">
    <memoryresize size0="2" size="4" />
    <determlimit value="1e-3" />
    <inoutzone>
        <refilling value="2" comment="Advanced for reverse flows" />
        <inputtreatment value="1" comment="Convert fluid (necessary for outlet)" />
        <layers value="4" />
        <zone2d>
           <particles mkfluid="2" direction="left" />
        </zone2d>
        <imposevelocity mode="2" comment="Extrapolated value" />
        <imposerhop mode="1" comment="Hydrostatic" />
        <imposezsurf mode="2" comment="Calculated from fluid domain">
            <zsurf0 value="0" comment="Initial Z-surface value" />
        </imposezsurf>
    </inoutzone>
</inout>

    Reverse-flow-capable refilling: refilling="2" (the advanced mode) is used because the flow direction near the gate can reverse as it oscillates, which the simpler refilling modes are not designed to handle robustly.

    Hydrostatic outlet density, dynamic surface: outlet density is imposed hydrostatically, while the outlet's z-surface is calculated directly from the evolving fluid domain rather than fixed, starting from an initial value of 0 m.

    Chrono FEA Coupling¶

    <chrono>
    <schemescale value="1" />
    <configfea>             
        <savedata value="0.0001">
            <start value="0" />
            <finish value="10" />
            <precision value="16" />
        </savedata>
        <savevtk value="true" />
        <floating mkbound="50"/>
    </configfea>        
    <collision activate="false" />          
    <nodes>
        <node ref="0" mass="0" fixed="false" x="0.1025" y="0.0" z="#baseOffset" comment="Start of beam"/>
        <node ref="1" mass="0" fixed="true" x="0.1025" y="0.0" z="#beamLength+baseOffset" comment="End of beam"/>
    </nodes>                
    <feafloating id="cylinder1" mkbound="50" massbody="0" type="1" modelfile="AutoActual">
        <beamEuler segments="40">
            <pointA noderef="0"/>
            <pointB noderef="1"/>
            <section shape="1">
              <_size x="#tbeam" y="1" z="#beamLength" />
              <width value="1" />
              <thickness value="#tbeam" />                      
              <density value="1100" />
              <poisson value="0.5" />
              <youngmodulus value="6.5e6" />
              <rgdamping value="0.02" />
            </section>          
        </beamEuler>
    </feafloating>  
</chrono>

    Clamped at the top, free at the bottom: node 1, at the top of the gate (z = beamLength + baseOffset), is fixed="true", anchoring the gate to the rigid wall above it, while node 0, near the floor, is free — the opposite arrangement to a standard cantilever, matching the way the physical gate hinges open at the bottom under load rather than at the top.

    High-precision node output: savedata writes node positions and forces to CSV every 0.0001 s, with 16 digits of precision, between t = 0 s and t = 10 s (beyond the actual simulated time, so effectively covering the whole run), which supports extracting a detailed gate-deflection time history for validation.

    Material properties: the gate has a density of 1100 kg/m³, Poisson ratio 0.5, Young's modulus 6.5 MPa and a Rayleigh damping ratio of 0.02 — parameters typical of a soft rubber-like gate material used in these hydroelastic benchmarks.

    Numerical Gauges¶

    <gauges>
    <default>                    
        <computedt value="0.001" />
        <output value="true" />
        <outputdt value="0.002" />                    
    </default>
    <swl name="S0">                    
        <pointdp coefdp="0.25" />
        <point0 x="0.092" y="0" z="0.0" />
        <point2 x="0.092" y="0" z="0.16" />
    </swl>
    <swl name="S1">                    
        <pointdp coefdp="0.25" />
        <point0 x="0.05" y="0" z="0.0" />
        <point2 x="0.05" y="0" z="0.16" />
    </swl>
    <swl name="S2">                    
        <pointdp coefdp="0.25" />
        <point0 x="0.08" y="0" z="0.0" />
        <point2 x="0.08" y="0" z="0.16" />
    </swl>
</gauges>

    Three vertical SWL gauges (S0, S1, S2), all inside the reservoir at \(x\) = 0.092, 0.05 and 0.08 m respectively, track the free-surface elevation from the tank floor up to 0.16 m as the water level responds to the gate's deflection. Measurements are computed every 0.001 s and written out every 0.002 s, matching typical probe locations used to validate this kind of hydroelastic gate experiment against physical measurements.

    Numerical Parameters¶

    Boundary Method¶

    <parameter key="Boundary" value="2" comment="mDBC" />
<parameter key="SlipMode" value="2" comment="No-slip" />
<parameter key="NoPenetration" value="0" comment="Off" />

    mDBC with no-slip is used at all rigid and flexible boundaries.

    Kernel and Viscosity¶

    <parameter key="Kernel" value="2" comment="Wendland" />
<parameter key="ViscoTreatment" value="3" comment="Laminar" />
<parameter key="Visco" value="1e-06" />
<parameter key="ViscoBoundFactor" value="1" />

    A purely laminar viscosity treatment is used with the physical kinematic viscosity of water, and the full boundary viscosity contribution is retained (ViscoBoundFactor="1"), unlike some of the other flexible-structure cases seen previously.

    Density Diffusion and Shifting¶

    <parameter key="DensityDT" value="3" comment="Fourtakas(full)" />
<parameter key="DensityDTvalue" value="0.1" />

    The full Fourtakas density diffusion term is active.

    Rigid Algorithm¶

    <parameter key="RigidAlgorithm" value="0" comment="Collision-free" />
<parameter key="FtPause" value="0.0" />

    RigidAlgorithm is set to 0 (collision-free) here, since the only floating body in the case (the gate) is defined entirely as a flexible FEA beam through the chrono/feafloating block rather than as a classic rigid floating.

    Simulation Time¶

    <parameter key="TimeMax" value="0.40" comment="Time of simulation" units_comment="seconds" />
<parameter key="TimeOut" value="0.002" comment="Time out data" units_comment="seconds" />

    The case runs for only 0.40 s, since the gate's deflection and the resulting water release happen very quickly once the simulation starts, with output written every 0.002 s.

    Running the Simulation¶

    Execute the case using the provided batch/shell script. As with other Chrono-coupled cases, this requires a Chrono-enabled build of DualSPHysics.

    Windows¶

    wCaseFlexibleGate_win64_GPU.bat
    REM Generate case geometry
gencase.exe CaseFlexibleGate_Def CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate -save:all

REM Run simulation on GPU (Chrono-enabled build)
dualsphysicsgpu.exe -gpu CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate/data

REM Convert to VTK format
partvtk.exe -dirdata CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate/data -savevtk post/PartFluid -onlytype:-all,+fluid

    Linux¶

    xCaseFlexibleGate_linux64_GPU.sh
    # Generate case geometry
./gencase CaseFlexibleGate_Def CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate -save:all

# Run simulation on GPU (Chrono-enabled build)
./dualsphysicsgpu -gpu CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate/data

# Convert to VTK format
./partvtk -dirdata CaseFlexibleGate_out/CaseFlexibleGate/data -savevtk post/PartFluid -onlytype:-all,+fluid

    Required input file: Floating_Materials.xml must be present alongside the case definition, since the boundary material properties are read from it via the propertyfile link.

    Results and Validation¶

    As the simulation starts, the hydrostatic and hydrodynamic pressure of the 0.14 m water column deflects the flexible gate, which is clamped at the top and free at the bottom, so its lower edge swings downstream, opening a gap through which water escapes into the initially dry downstream channel and out through the open outlet boundary. The three SWL gauges (S0, S1, S2) record the resulting drop and oscillation of the free surface at three positions within the reservoir, while the Chrono savedata output provides the gate's node positions and internal forces over time. Together, these outputs allow both the free-surface response and the gate's own deflection history to be compared against reference data from flexible-gate dam-break experiments, such as those of Antoci, Gallati & Sibilla (2007), which this case configuration closely follows.

    References¶

    ◆ Antoci, C., Gallati, M., & Sibilla, S. (2007). Numerical simulation of fluid-structure interaction by SPH. Computers & Structures, 85(11-14), 879-890.
